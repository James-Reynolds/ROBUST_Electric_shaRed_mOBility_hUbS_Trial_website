# Project manager

# Principal Investigator


[Prof Brian Caulfield](https://www.tcd.ie/research/profiles/?profile=caulfib) is a Professor in the Department of Civil, Structural and Environmental Engineering, Trinity College Dublin. His background is in Transport Science and he has been a part of several large projects that have looked at behavioural change in transport. He has used many analysis techniques to measure the impacts of these changes such as stated preference, cluster analysis, nudging techniques and conducted many field trials. He has been active in transport research for over 10 years and to date has attracted €3.2m in research funding from both National and EU funding sources. In 2017, he addressed the Irish Citizens Assembly on Climate Change, providing evidence on what low carbon transport options Ireland could adopt to meet emission reduction targets and in 2020 provided evidence to the Climate Change Advisory Council on transport and climate change. He is currently on an OECD working group examining rural connectivity. He has widely published on transport and its impacts on the environment, he has published over 170 journal and conference papers and has a H factor of 30 and his work has been cited over 3,500 times. 


# Other Research team members

The transdisciplinary project team comprises of academics from civil engineering, computer science psychology and economics. The team is supported by several public and private organisations to conduct the ROBUST project trial and ensure the impact of the trial is realised.

## Trinity College Dublin (TCD)


[Prof O’Mahony](https://www.tcd.ie/civileng/people/mmmahony/)holds the Chair of Civil Engineering and is Director of the Centre for Transport Research at TCD. With 30 years of transport research experience, she has been coordinator or PI on several EU and nationally funded research projects including GREEN eMOTION (involving detailed statistical analysis of a very large and complicated electric vehicle multi-country database). She also analysed the data for the ESB eCar project on which members of the public were given an EV for a period to use in a trial. Key areas of interest and experience include influencing transport behaviour and impacts of transport on the environment. She is currently leading a project funded by the EPA that investigates the impact of NO2 on health. She has generated ~€7million in research income for the Centre on projects worth a total value of ~€30m. She is author of ~200 publications, has over 4,500 citations and a H-index of 32.

[Dr Cromie](https://psychology.tcd.ie/people/sdcromie) is Director of the Centre for Innovative Human Systems in TCD, a research centre dedicated to understanding and enhancing the human element of complex industrial and societal systems. He has over 26 years’ experience of action research, training and consultancy in transport, healthcare, aviation, pharmaceutical, process, manufacturing and maritime sectors and has worked with organisations such as Irish Rail, AECOM, Aer Lingus, Rolls Royce, Airbus, British Airways, SAS, Cathay Pacific, EASA, Pfizer, BP & Statoil. He has brought in over €2m of research funding from diverse sources including the EU, SFI and HRB. The focus of his research has been on developing a sophisticated understanding of the application of human factors, occupational health and safety management principles in real operational situations. He has been PI on several EU-funded projects: ManuVar, InnHF, STAMINA BPM and lead TCD researcher on others: STAMINA, STAMP, AITRAM. His most relevant recent project is SMILE – Smart Mobility Initiative for Lower Emissions – funded under the concept phase of the SFI Zero-Emissions Challenge.


[Prof Owen Conlan](https://www.scss.tcd.ie/Owen.Conlan/) is a Professor in Computer Science at the School of Computer Science and Statistics at TCD. He is a leading member of the personalisation research community having authored 180+ scientific peer-reviewed papers and international journal articles (h-index of 29 (Google Scholar); 3100+ citations). Owen is recognised for his ground-breaking work on developing advanced personalisation systems, empowering user understanding of complex data and rich media, and in developing appropriate user control over adaptive systems. He has led several large-scale research projects and has been competitively awarded ~€12m in research funding to support his research from a range of funding programmes: European Commission (Framework programmes, including Horizon Europe, H2020 and Marie Skłodowska-Curie), Science Foundation Ireland (AMAS, CNGL, ADAPT, ADAPT II), Enterprise Ireland, Irish Research Council and Industry. He is a co-applicant, co-PI and Strand Leader of the Science Foundation Ireland ADAPT research centre and in the recently awarded ADAPT II proposal (securing €32 million in funding for the centre from 2021 to 2026). Owen leads the Digitally Enhanced Engagement strand which is underpinned by his research vision for personalised proactive agency, with a responsibility for a budget of €5 million (representing a third of the centre’s research platform budget).

After completing Masters degrees at the Institute of Transport Studies at Monash University, he moved to Toronto and worked in transport planning at BA Group, in the early 2010s. James' PhD project was one of 18 in the SEPT-GRIP, half of which were funded by government departments or transit operators. After completing the PhD in late 2022, James continued as a Research Fellow within the Public Transport Research Group (PTRG) at Monash University until 2025, prior to commencing a post-doctoral position at ATU as part of the ROBUST: Electric shaRed mOBility hUbS Trial. 


## Others

[Dr Agnieszka Stefaniec](https://www.southampton.ac.uk/people/65zjrz/doctor-agnieszka-stefaniec) is a Lecturer in Management Science in the Department of Decision Analytics and Risk at Southampton Business School, University of Southampton. Prior to her current role, Dr Stefaniec was a Postdoctoral Research Fellow in the Department of Civil, Structural and Environmental Engineering at Trinity College Dublin, Ireland, where she contributed to the ROBUST project and the 5YAR project (Ireland’s 5-Year Assessment Report on Climate Change). She holds a PhD in Management Science and Engineering, MEng in Architecture, and BEng in Architecture and Urban Planning. Her research interests lie in the area of sustainable urban development with a focus on equitable transportation systems and inclusive urbanization. Most recently, she is exploring opportunities from low-carbon transformations and just transition. She has published in Transportation Research Part D: Transport and the Environment, Energy, and others, and has presented her work at multiple international conferences.
